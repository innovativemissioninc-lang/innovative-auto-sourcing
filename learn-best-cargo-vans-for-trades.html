Best Commercial Cargo Vans for GTA Trades: Transit vs. ProMaster vs. Express

Ford Transit, Ram ProMaster, and Chevrolet Express each suit a different kind of trade. Here's how to match the chassis to the job before you buy.

These three platforms cover the vast majority of cargo vans on GTA roads, and each one has a genuinely different layout that suits different trades. The "best" one depends on what you're carrying and how you're carrying it, not brand preference.

Ford Transit (T-250 / T-350 High Roof)

The most common high-roof cargo van in the GTA fleet market. High-roof trims offer genuine stand-up cargo space, fuel economy is competitive for the class, and newer model years offer an available AWD option. A strong general-purpose choice for HVAC, electrical, and delivery fleets that need standing room and don't require extreme payload capacity.

Ram ProMaster

Front-wheel-drive layout gives the ProMaster the widest, most square cargo box of the three and the lowest load floor — a real advantage for trades moving bulky material in and out repeatedly, like plumbing supply or cabinetry. FWD also tends to handle predictably in snow because of weight distribution over the drive wheels, though there's no AWD option on this platform.

Chevrolet Express / GMC Savana

The traditional body-on-frame, rear-wheel-drive van. Generally the strongest tow rating and payload capacity of the three, and mechanically simple — but a lower roof and narrower cargo area than Transit or ProMaster in most trims, and RWD needs good winter tires or added weight to handle well in snow.

Matching the chassis to your trade

  • HVAC and electrical contractors who need to stand upright while working from the van generally lean Transit.
  • Plumbing and cabinetry trades moving bulky, low-profile material generally lean ProMaster.
  • Delivery and towing-heavy fleets generally lean Express.

The sourcing takeaway

All three platforms move through the same closed dealer-auction channel, across a wide range of pricing depending on year, mileage, and trim. Since none of them is reliably the cheapest option across the board, the right call comes down to matching the chassis to your actual trade — not assuming one badge is always the better deal.
