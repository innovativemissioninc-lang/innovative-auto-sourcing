FINANCING & LOANS

The Auto Loan Math Many Buyers Never Check

Two loans with the same advertised rate can cost very different amounts. The difference usually comes down to how the interest is calculated, and whether anything else got quietly rolled into the balance.

When two loan offers show the same interest rate, they can still result in very different total costs. The gap usually isn't the rate itself -- it's what else got folded into the loan, and how the interest is actually applied to the balance over time.

Financed extras add up fast

Extended warranties, paint and fabric protection, GAP insurance, admin fees, and etching charges are all routinely rolled into the loan amount rather than paid upfront. That's not necessarily bad -- some of these products have real value -- but every dollar added to the loan principal accrues interest for the entire term. A $2,000 add-on financed over 72 months at 8% doesn't just cost $2,000; it costs $2,000 plus however much interest that extra principal generates over six years.

Ask what's actually in the "amount financed" line

Every proper Canadian loan disclosure has a line for the amount financed, separate from the vehicle price. If that number is meaningfully higher than the negotiated vehicle price minus your down payment and trade equity, something else has been added -- and you're entitled to know exactly what, and to decline anything you don't want financed.

Simple interest vs. how payments are applied

Most Canadian auto loans use simple interest calculated on the declining balance, which is buyer-friendly: extra payments go straight to principal and reduce future interest. Read your contract to confirm this is how yours works, and ask specifically whether making an extra payment reduces your balance immediately or gets held and applied differently -- the answer should be in writing, not just told to you verbally.

A five-minute check before you sign anything

  • Compare the "amount financed" to the vehicle price you agreed on -- flag any gap.
  • Ask for the total interest cost over the full term in dollars.
  • Confirm whether any add-on products are financed or paid separately, and whether they're optional.
  • Confirm there's no prepayment penalty if you want the flexibility to pay it off faster.

This kind of scrutiny works at any dealer, on any financing offer -- it's just basic contract literacy that protects you regardless of where you buy.
